# Stages of competence

Source: https://scottjeffrey.com/four-stages-of-learning/ , plus Dreyfus (novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, expert) as a companion model where rules give way to experience.

| Stage | State | Coaching moves | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Unconscious incompetence | Doesn't know what they don't know | Show the whole map; give a small task they will likely get wrong, to expose the gap | "I've seen it, so I get it" (Dunning-Kruger flavour) |
| 2 Conscious incompetence | Knows they're bad at it | Decompose into the smallest sub-skill; worked example, then near-identical retry; fast feedback; name the stage aloud to turn irritation into curiosity; track small wins | Quitting; self-blame. This is where most people drop out |
| 3 Conscious competence | Can do it with concentration | Volume of deliberate practice at the edge of ability; feedback after they attempt; vary the problems; treat plateaus as consolidation | Plateau, skipping fundamentals, burnout |
| 4 Unconscious competence | Automatic, in flow | Edge cases, trade-offs, harder and weirder problems, teach-back; trust the trained skill | Stagnation; cannot explain how they do it |
| 5 Teaching from mastery (optional) | Can explain *how* and teach it | Write, mentor, document; check for expert blind spot | Forgetting what novices find hard |

Progression triggers (from the article): 1 to 2 needs a teacher, a failure or honest self-reflection. 2 to 3 is the stall point: break it down, tighten feedback loops, expect plateaus, track wins. 3 to 4 is not "train harder" alone; it comes with letting go and trusting the trained skill.

Useful rituals: say "I'm in conscious incompetence on X" aloud; end-of-day "what did I learn, what will I repeat tomorrow?"

## Caveats (say these if the user asks how solid the model is)

- Origin is disputed. Commonly credited to Noel Burch (Gordon Training International, 1970s); the article cites Martin Broadwell (1969, an unpublished handout), which I could not verify; the article's author says he did not create it.
- Its neuroscience claims, stage timelines (stage 2 hours-weeks, 3 weeks-months, 4 months-years) and fifth stage are uncited and informal.
- Treat it as a diagnostic heuristic for choosing the next move, not validated science. The techniques that carry the evidence are in `techniques.md`.
